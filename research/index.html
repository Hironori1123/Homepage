RESEARCH

Research

My research examines how phonological grammar interacts with lexical knowledge, morphological structure, and linguistic experience. I approach these questions using experimental, corpus-based, and computational methods.

Jump to publications ↓

01

Lexical strata & morphological representation

My current research asks how differences in morphological and lexical representation contribute to phonological patterns across lexical strata in Japanese. In collaboration with Jie Zhang, I investigate accentuation in Sino-Japanese compounds, focusing on how speakers learn and generalize accentual tendencies associated with individual morphemes.

Using novel-word experiments and computational modeling, we examine how the familiarity of constituent characters affects these generalizations. This work explores whether differences between Sino-Japanese and loanword accentuation can be understood in terms of morphological representation under a shared phonological grammar.

Related work: Katsuda & Zhang (in preparation).

02

Variation, language contact & bilingual phonology

I investigate how phonological patterns are shaped by variation, language contact, and speakers' linguistic experience. In my work on Japanese loanword accentuation, I examine how Japanese phonological constraints interact with English stress patterns and speakers' generalizations about English stress.

With collaborators, I have also studied frequency-conditioned variation in voiced velar nasalization in Japanese, combining corpus analysis, experiments, and formal modeling. Another line of collaborative work examines how bilingual speakers apply phonological processes across language boundaries during Spanish–English code-switching.

Related work: Katsuda (2025); Breiss, Katsuda & Kawahara (2026); Katsuda, Puigdelliura-Repiso & Zuraw (2025).

03

Speech production, perception & lexical processing

My phonetic and psycholinguistic research explores how prosody and segmental information affect speech production and perception. This includes work on how Japanese listeners perceive vowel length, the roles of pitch accent and segmental cues in word recognition, and speaking-rate effects on vowel and consonant length contrasts.

I have also collaborated on studies of phonotactic learning in English-learning infants. In my current postdoctoral position, I contribute to experimental research on lexical representation and processing in te reo Māori.

Related work: Steffman & Katsuda (2021); Katsuda & Steffman (2022); Katsuda & Sundara (2024); Katsuda & Kang (2026).
