RESEARCH

Research

My research examines how phonological grammar interacts with lexical knowledge, morphological structure, and linguistic experience. I approach these questions using experimental, corpus-based, and computational methods.

Jump to publications ↓

01

Japanese accentuation & lexical representations

A central goal of my work is to understand how Japanese speakers learn and generalize accent patterns. In my research on Japanese loanwords, I examine how Japanese phonological constraints interact with information about the source language, including English stress patterns and generalizations about English stress.

In ongoing collaborative work on Sino-Japanese compounds, I investigate how morpheme-specific accent patterns are generalized to new words and how the familiarity of individual characters influences the strength of morphological representations and their accentual effects.

Related work: Katsuda (2025), Phonology.

02

Variation, language contact & bilingual phonology

I investigate the sources of probabilistic patterns in phonology. With collaborators, I have studied frequency-conditioned variation in voiced velar nasalization in Japanese, combining corpus analysis, experiments, and formal modeling.

Another line of collaborative work examines how bilingual speakers apply phonological processes across language boundaries during Spanish–English code-switching.

Related work: Breiss, Katsuda & Kawahara (2026); Katsuda, Puigdelliura-Repiso & Zuraw (2025).

03

Speech production, perception & lexical processing

My phonetic and psycholinguistic research explores how prosody and segmental information affect speech production and perception. This includes work on how Japanese listeners perceive vowel length, the roles of pitch accent and segmental cues in word recognition, and speaking-rate effects on vowel and consonant length contrasts.

I have also collaborated on studies of phonotactic learning in English-learning infants. In my current postdoctoral position, I contribute to experimental research on lexical representation and processing in te reo Māori.

Related work: Steffman & Katsuda (2021); Katsuda & Steffman (2022); Katsuda & Sundara (2024); Katsuda & Kang (2026).
